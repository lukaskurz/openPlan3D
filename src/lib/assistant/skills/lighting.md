# Residential lighting

Work like a lighting designer, not a lamp catalogue. Start from how each room is used and where the furniture is, plan light in layers, place each light against the furniture or wall it serves, then decide the switching. Residential lighting is not covered by a norm in Germany or Austria (DIN EN 12464-1 covers workplaces only, so it applies to a real home-office desk). The numbers below come from licht.de and ERCO or are rules of thumb (RoT). Lengths are in cm from the finished floor; H is the room's ceiling height.

## 1. Read the room first
- get_plan: ceiling height per room, windows, doors, and the furniture: sofa and TV or projector wall, dining table, worktops, bed, desk, mirror, wardrobes, artwork.
- Small closed "rooms" of about 0.1–1.5 m² between walls are often chimneys, shafts or solid masonry. Ask what they are, and leave solid parts out of the lighting.
- Things you can't see but that decide the plan (ask, or assume and say so): bare concrete slab or suspended ceiling, and how deep it is (GU10 spots need a cavity of about 15 cm or more; a 5–7 cm cavity only takes flat LED downlights; bare concrete takes surface spots, track or pendants). Old buildings in Austria/Germany often have timber-beam ceilings with reed plaster: fix pendants, fans and projectors to beams or blocking, and use spots rated for flammable surroundings. Also ask who uses the room, for what (reading, homework, cinema), and whether it's a rental or a renovation.

## 2. Layers
| Layer | German | Fixtures |
|---|---|---|
| Ambient | Grundlicht | ceiling light, wide downlights, coves, uplighters, wall lights |
| Task | Zonen-/Platzlicht | pendant over the table, under-cabinet light, reading lamp, mirror light, bedside light |
| Accent and mood | Akzentlicht | spots on art, shelf light, table lamps, wall washers, light behind the TV |
- Every habitable room gets ambient and task light; living, dining and bedrooms also get accent light. A living room of about 20 m² needs at least 3 light sources, better 5, at different heights, with 2–3 of them dimmable. A kitchen needs at least 2. One central ceiling light alone is always wrong.
- Order: task light first, at the furniture; then ambient light, aimed at walls and ceiling rather than the floor; then accent light. Each layer on its own circuit (RoT).

## 3. Key numbers
| What | Rule |
|---|---|
| Downlight distance from wall | ≈ H/3 (≈ 85 at H 258). Kitchen: about 60 without wall cabinets, about 100 with them (just in front of the fronts) |
| Downlight spacing | ≈ 2 × wall distance (80–120 for 36–60° spots at H 250–270). ERCO for even general light: spacing ≤ 1.5 × (H − 85), wall distance half the spacing, corner lights on the 45° line |
| Wall washers | ≈ H/3 from the wall, spaced ≤ 1.3 × that distance, at least 3 per wall |
| Spot on art | 30° from vertical: distance from wall = (H − picture centre) × 0.58 (≈ 58 for H 260 and centre 160) |
| Beam diameter | D = 2 × height × tan(beam/2): 36° from 250 up lights a circle of about 160; 60° about 290 |
| Dining pendant | bottom ≈ 60 above the tabletop (≈ 135 from the floor for a 75 table; up to 80 above it if H > 270); width ½–⅔ of the table width; centred on the table, not the room |
| Linear pendant | ½–⅔ of the table length, at least 15 shorter than the table at each end |
| Island pendants | 75–90 above the counter (≈ 165–180); 2–3 pendants 60–90 apart over ⅔ of the island length |
| Anything hanging over a walkway | bottom ≥ 210 |
| Wall light, living room or hall | centre 150–180, above shoulder height; in halls 180–300 apart |
| Bedside wall light | 76–91 above the mattress top (≈ 140–165); over the nightstand; one per side, each switched on its own |
| Mirror | two identical lights left and right at ≈ 160, about 10 outside the mirror edge; for a wide mirror one more above at 190–200. Never spots aimed at the mirror |
| Reading lamp | beside and slightly behind the seat, shade bottom ≈ 100–107; for writing, on the side opposite the writing hand |
| Wardrobe | wide-beam downlights 50–80 in front of it |
| Illuminance | kitchen ≥ 500 lx, worktop 500–650; bathroom ≥ 300; desk and reading 500; children's room 300–500; living 100–300, dimmable; bedroom 100–150; hall 100; stairs 150–200 |
| Lumen budget (RoT) | living, bedroom, hall: 100–150 lm/m²; kitchen, bathroom, study: 250–300 lm/m². A 60-year-old needs about twice the light of a 20-year-old |
| Colour | 2700 K in living rooms, bedrooms, dining and halls; 3000 K in kitchens (worktop up to 4000 K) and bathrooms (or tunable); office 3000–4000 K. Ra ≥ 90 at the dining table, mirror and kitchen. One product family per room; dimmable LEDs on matching dimmers |

## 4. Room recipes
- **Living**: ambient from wall washers, an uplighter or a cove; a reading lamp at the reading seat; accents on art and shelves; table lamps on the sideboard; a switched outlet for the lamps. TV: a light behind the screen, low ambient light on, nothing that reflects in the screen from the seats. Projector: nothing on the screen wall, no downlight in the beam.
- **Dining**: pendant(s) centred on the table, dimmable; the table is the brightest zone but not the only lit one (add a wall washer or a sideboard lamp).
- **Kitchen**: at least 2 ambient sources off the centre, or a row of downlights 60 or 100 from the wall (see above); the worktop itself lit by under-cabinet lights shielded to the front; the hob lit by the hood; island pendants as above. In an open kitchen-living room, separate scenes but one colour temperature.
- **Bedroom**: dimmable ambient light, switchable from the door and from the bed (two-way switching); bedside lights; wardrobe light; no floor lamp in the night path to the door or bathroom; an optional low orientation light (≤ 2700 K, motion sensor).
- **Bathroom**: IP-rated ambient light ≥ 300 lx; two mirror lights at the sides; a wide downlight over the shower or tub; a fan switched with the light in a windowless room.
- **Hall**: 100 lx, light on walls and ceiling; narrow halls (< 150 wide) one row on the centre line, about 100–120 apart; switches at both ends if it is longer than 3 m, or a motion sensor.
- **Stairs**: broad light from above at the top landing; step lights about every third tread, low and aimed at the tread; switches at the top and bottom.
- **Home office**: ≥ 500 lx on the desk from the side (from the left for right-handers); no reflections in the screen; not sitting with the back to the window.
- **Children's room**: 300–500 lx, dimmable; desk lamp opposite the writing hand; light and switch reachable from the bed; night light; 230 V lamps out of reach of small children.

## 5. Bathroom zones (Germany: DIN VDE 0100-701; Austria: OVE E 8101, part 7-701)
| Zone | Extent | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | inside the tub or shower tray | IPX7; only fixed extra-low-voltage equipment (SELV ≤ 12 V AC / 30 V DC) |
| 1 | above the tub or tray up to 225 from the floor; a level shower without tray: 120 radius around the fixed shower outlet | IPX4 (IPX5 where water jets are used); only fixed, permanently connected equipment such as luminaires, fans, water heaters |
| 2 | a 60 band beyond zone 1, up to 225 | IPX4; no 230 V outlets except shaver outlets |
Keep switches and outlets out of zones 0–2, and use IP44 near the washbasin (RoT). All bathroom circuits are protected by an RCD of 30 mA or less. For more on bathroom electrics, read the electrical guide.

## 6. Switching
- Switches at 105, inside the room, on the latch side of the door (the side the door opens from, away from the hinges), about 15 from the frame, never behind the opening door leaf. Barrier-free: 85–105 (see the accessibility guide).
- Two-way switching for rooms with two doors, halls longer than 3 m, stairs, and bedrooms (door and bed). Three or more points: an intermediate switch or push-buttons with a relay.
- Separate switches or circuits per layer; a switched outlet near the door for floor and table lamps; an outlet wherever a floor lamp stands.
- RAL-RG 678 lighting outlets at levels 1 / 2 / 3 (1 is the DIN 18015-2 minimum): kitchen 2/3/3; bathroom 2/3/3; WC 1/1/2; living ≤ 20 m² 2/2/3, > 20 m² 3/3/4; dining 1/1/2; bedroom, children's room or study ≤ 20 m² 1/2/3, > 20 m² 2/3/4; hall ≤ 3 m 1/2/2, > 3 m 2/2/2 (switched from at least two places). A room with a dining corner gets one more.

## 7. How to do it in openPlan3D
1. get_plan, then search_catalog with category Lighting. The catalog has `ceiling_light` (40×40×15, flat on the ceiling; set width/depth for its diameter), `recessed_light` (spot or downlight), `pendant_light` (30 high, default drop 60), `chandelier` (50 high, drop 30), `ceiling_fan`, `wall_sconce` (wall item, 20 high, default 170 up), `floor_lamp` and `table_lamp` (floor items, Furnishing trade). Don't use the Electrical symbols (`sym_ceiling_light`, `sym_recessed_light`, `sym_pendant`): they are 2D only and don't light the 3D view. Missing: track spots, LED strips and coves, under-cabinet lights, picture lights, uplighters. Approximate them (a row of `recessed_light` close together for a strip, a narrow `wall_sconce` for a picture light, `floor_lamp` for an uplighter) and say what you approximated.
2. Ceiling items hang from the room's own ceiling: drop is the distance from the ceiling down to the item's top. For a target bottom height: drop = ceiling height − bottom height − item height. Example: ceiling 258, table 75, pendant bottom 60 above it (135): drop = 258 − 135 − 30 = 93. Spots and flat ceiling lights take drop 0. If the person changes a room's ceiling height (update_elements ceilingHeight), work out the pendant drops again.
3. Pendant positions: take the table or island centre from get_plan and place the pendant at the same x, y; for 2–3 pendants, space them along the long axis of the table (rotation tells you which way it runs).
4. Spot rows: work out the positions from the room corners (wall distance, then spacing), lined up with walls, worktop fronts or the table axis, and place them all in one place_furniture call. Keep them off the space above seats, the bed pillows and the screen wall.
5. Wall lights and switches: wall items snap to the wall face nearest x, y and face into the room on the side x, y is on; elevation is the height of the item's bottom. To hit an exact spot, take a point on the wall's centre line (start + t × direction) and step thickness/2 + 5 into the room, or pass wallId. A sconce centred at 170 needs elevation 160.
6. Table lamps: elevation is the height of the surface they stand on (nightstand ≈ 55, sideboard 75–90), and x, y on that piece of furniture. Floor lamps stand beside the seat, out of walkways.
7. Switches: search_catalog "switch" gives `sym_switch` (wall, 105). Place one at the latch side of each entrance; if you can't tell which side the hinges are on, look with view_plan (the door swing is drawn) or choose the side where the wall continues, and say so. Then link_switch(switchId, [light ids]). For two-way switching, place two switches and link both to the same lights. Floor and table lamps are Furnishing, not Electrical: switch them through the outlet they plug into (place `sym_outlet` near the lamp, then link the switch to that outlet).
8. Set each fixture's output with `light` in place_furniture or update_elements. get_plan and search_catalog show what each fixture gives now, e.g. "light 800 lm, 2700 K, beam 100°", with "(set: …)" listing the fields you changed. Fields you leave out keep the catalog's; null in a field, or light: null, goes back to it.
   - Catalog defaults, all 2700 K: `recessed_light` 400 lm, beam 38°; `ceiling_light` 1500 lm, 130°; `pendant_light` 800 lm, 100°; `chandelier` 1600 lm, all round; `wall_sconce` 400 lm, half up and half down, 100°; `floor_lamp` 2000 lm, mostly up; `table_lamp` 400 lm, all round; `ceiling_fan` 800 lm.
   - lumens (full output): take the room's total from the lumen budget in section 3 (lm/m² × floor area) and split it over the ambient fixtures, so their sum matches. Task lights come on top: a pendant over a dining table 600–1000 lm, worktop light 300–500 lm per metre of worktop, a reading lamp 400–800 lm.
   - colorTemperature (K): as in the Colour row of section 3. Use 2700 in living rooms, bedrooms, dining and halls, and 3000 in kitchens and bathrooms (worktop task light up to 4000). Use 2200–2400 for mood and orientation lights. Keep one value per room unless a task light needs to be cooler.
   - beamAngle (spots only: `recessed_light`, `ceiling_light`, `pendant_light`, `wall_sconce`, `floor_lamp`, `ceiling_fan`; 5–140°): 36–40° for normal downlights, 24° or narrower for accents on art, 60° or wider for an even field in halls and in front of wardrobes. Check the spacing with the beam diameter rule in section 3.
   - tilt (spots only, 0–90° from straight down towards the item's front): 15–30° to wash a wall, about 30° for artwork. The front follows rotation as for furniture (0 faces +y, down the plan; 90 faces −x; 180 faces −y; 270 faces +x), so turn the spot to face the wall it should light.
   - dim (0 off to 1 full): a dimmer setting, not the installed output. Use it when the person asks for a scene, such as evening with ambient light at 0.3–0.5 and the pendant at 0.6, and say that the installed output is the lumens.

## 8. Check before you answer
- view_3d of each room you lit (room: name), and an eye-level view from the door (from: the doorway, to: the room centre): pendants at the right height and over the table, nothing hanging into walkways below 210, sconces on the intended wall face and side of the bed or mirror, no spots above seats or the bed pillows. The fixtures light the 3D view with their own lumens, beam and colour, but daylight lights it too, so judge where the light falls rather than how bright it is.
- Every room you touched has more than one light source (except small storage rooms and WCs) and at least one switch at its entrance, linked to its lights; rooms with two entrances or halls longer than 3 m have two switches.
- Bathroom: no outlet or switch in zones 0–2; lights in zones 1–2 are IP-rated (say so).
- No light reflecting in the TV or projection screen from the seats; nothing in the projector beam.
- The ambient fixtures' lumens add up to the room's lumen budget, and each room keeps one colour temperature except deliberate task or mood lights.
- In your short reply: the layers per room, the key heights, switches and light settings, and anything you approximated or assumed, such as the ceiling construction.
