# Outlets, switches and connections

Plan the electrics from how each room is furnished: an outlet where something plugs in, a switch at every entrance and at every bed. Heights in cm from the finished floor; RoT marks a rule of thumb rather than a norm value. Germany: DIN 18015-1/-2/-3 (minimum equipment and installation zones) and RAL-RG 678 (equipment levels). Austria: OVE E 8015 (July 2022, replacing ÖVE/ÖNORM E 8015-2:2006) defines minimum, standard and comfort variants with its own counts, which aren't reproduced here; Austrian practice is close to the German values, so use the counts below as orientation for Austria and say that you did. The electrician decides circuits, cable routes and protection.

## 1. Equipment levels
RAL-RG 678 (edition November 2010, as summarised by HEA in 2011; check the current edition for binding numbers) has three levels for a whole flat: 1 = minimum as in DIN 18015-2, 2 = standard, 3 = comfort (plus 1plus/2plus/3plus with building automation). Say which level you planned. For a family planning its own home, level 2 is a sensible default; level 1 is what you get when nothing better is agreed.

- Outlets at beds and above kitchen and utility worktops are at least double outlets, but each double counts as one in the table. In the bathroom one outlet may be combined with the mirror light. A room with a dining corner gets one more outlet and one more lighting outlet.
- Data and TV: level 1 has a telephone/data and a radio/TV/data connection in the living room and in bedrooms/study; levels 2–3 add more (up to 2–3 in a large living room). Practical: a double network outlet and TV outlet at the TV wall, at every desk and in every bedroom; empty conduits to every room; a media distribution box near the main panel.
- Circuits at level 1, by living area: ≤ 50 m² 3; 50–75 m² 4; 75–100 m² 5; 100–125 m² 6; > 125 m² 7. Levels 2–3 give rooms their own circuits instead. Extra appliances (sauna, underfloor heating in the bathroom, steam oven) need more.
- Appliances with their own circuit even when plugged in: cooker/hob, oven, microwave, dishwasher, washing machine, dryer, steam oven, water heater, heaters. Fridge and freezer: their own outlets (2 in the kitchen). One connection for the hood, one for a fan in a bathroom or WC without a window (switched with the light, with run-on).
- Every bed place and every room entrance gets a switching point (RAL-RG 678).

## 2. Installation zones (DIN 18015-3)
Concealed cables run only horizontally or vertically inside these zones, so nobody drills into them later; place outlets and switches in the zones.
- Horizontal: upper zone 15–45 below the ceiling (preferred 30); lower zone 15–45 above the floor (preferred 30); middle zone 100–130 (preferred 115), only in rooms with worktops (kitchen, utility).
- Vertical: 10–30 beside door and window openings and beside room corners (preferred 15 from the edge).
- No zones are defined for floors and ceilings.

| Desk | outlets and data at 30 below the desk, or just above the desktop (about 85) |

## 4. Room by room
- **Kitchen**: hob (and freestanding cooker) on a 400 V cooker connection box near the floor behind the base unit; built-in appliances each on their own circuit; at least two double outlets along the worktop, more on long runs (RoT: one about every 120 of worktop, none within about 30 of the sink edge or above the hob); outlets on the island need a floor connection, plan them before the screed. See the kitchen guide.
- **Bathroom**: zones from DIN VDE 0100-701 (Austria: OVE E 8101, part 7-701). Zone 0 is inside the tub or tray, zone 1 above it up to 225 (a level shower without tray: 120 around the outlet), zone 2 a further 60. No outlets or switches in zones 0–2 (only shaver outlets in zone 2); outlets beside the basin at about 105–120 and at least 60 from the tub or shower edge; the light switch outside, next to the door, or inside on the latch side outside the zones. All bathroom circuits on a 30 mA RCD. More in the lighting and bathroom guides.
- **Living room**: outlets at the TV wall, beside the sofa ends (lamps, charging), at the reading chair, at every wall longer than about 3 m; one switched outlet for floor and table lamps.
- **Bedroom**: double outlet and switch on both sides of a double bed; outlets at the desk and dressing table; the ceiling light switched from the door and from the bed.
- **Hall**: switches at every door into it if it is long; an outlet for the vacuum cleaner about every 5 m of hall (RoT); outlet and light for the wardrobe.
- **Smoke detectors**: Germany: required in bedrooms, children's rooms and halls that lead out of the flat. Austria (OIB-RL 2, new buildings): in every habitable room except kitchens, and in halls on escape routes. Ceiling, near the room centre, at least 50 from walls and lights (RoT). Catalog: `smoke_detector` (3D) or `sym_smoke` (2D).
- Outlet circuits in homes are protected by RCDs of 30 mA or less in both Germany and Austria; arc-fault detection (AFDD) is recommended for bedrooms and children's rooms.

## 5. Switches and doors
- On the latch side of the door (the side it opens from, away from the hinges), inside the room, about 15 beside the opening, at 105, never behind the opening door leaf. With a sliding or pocket door, on the side the door slides away from.
- Two-way switching: rooms with two doors, halls longer than 3 m, stairs (top and bottom), bedrooms (door and bed). Three or more points: intermediate switches or push-buttons.
- One switch per lighting layer or group; group switches in one frame, the general light nearest the door.

## 6. How to do it in openPlan3D
1. get_plan; search_catalog "outlet switch" (category Electrical): `sym_outlet` (wall, 30), `sym_outlet_double` (wall, 30), `sym_switch` (wall, 105), `sym_junction`, `smoke_detector`, `sym_smoke`. These are plan symbols (2D), and search_catalog only lists symbols when you search with words, not by category alone. Lights themselves come from the Lighting category (see the lighting guide).
2. Wall items snap to the wall face nearest x, y and face the side of the wall x, y is on; elevation is the mounting height. To put one at a point: take the wall's start and end from get_plan, the point at distance t along it, and step thickness/2 + 5 into the room; or pass wallId. Beside a door: t = the door's offset ± (door width/2 + 15).
3. Place all outlets and switches of a room in one place_furniture call, with elevation where it differs from the default (worktop 115, bedside 75, bathroom basin 110).
4. Door hinge side: get_plan doesn't say it directly; view_plan shows the swing arc. If you can't tell, put the switch on the side where the wall continues rather than into a corner, and say so.
5. link_switch(switchId, targetIds) for every switch: its lights, a ceiling fan, or a switched outlet. Two-way: two switches linked to the same lights. Floor and table lamps can't be linked themselves; link the outlet they plug into.
6. Fixed appliances (hob, oven, dishwasher, washer) don't need outlet symbols for the count, but say in your reply which ones need their own circuit or a 400 V connection.

## 7. Check before you answer
- Count each room's outlets and lighting outlets against the level you chose (say which); add the missing ones.
- Every room entrance has a switch on the latch side, linked to the room's lights (get_plan lists what each switch controls); bedrooms also at the bed.
- No outlet or switch in bathroom zones 0–2 or within about 30 of a sink; worktop outlets at about 115, not behind the hob.
- No outlet or switch hidden behind a wardrobe, a tall unit or the opening door leaf: look with view_3d (room) or view_plan.
- In your reply: the level you planned to, anything Austrian law may handle differently, and that the electrician confirms circuits.
